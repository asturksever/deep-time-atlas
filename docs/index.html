Sources and limits

Each piece of continental crust from an open plate model is draped with today's satellite imagery, then turned on the globe by its own plate's rotation for the age you pick. Between the pieces is ocean floor that has since been destroyed.

What the surface shows

The colours are today's surface carried back in time. Ice, deserts, forests and coastlines are modern, so an icy Antarctica sitting near the equator 500 million years ago is a reminder of that, not a claim about the past. Ancient shorelines, seas and mountains are not modelled.

Cities before their ground existed

Some cities stand on crust that the model only creates later, for example the mountain belt under Rio de Janeiro, which forms as Gondwana assembles about 520 million years ago. Before that age the city shows as a hollow ring labelled with the age its crust appears.

Poles

N and S mark Earth's spin axis. The model keeps the axis fixed and moves the plates across it, which is how palaeomagnetism records ancient latitude.

Uncertainty

Before about 250 Ma, palaeomagnetism constrains latitude only, so east-west positions are the model's best fit. Pacific absolute positions between 250 and 83 Ma are adjusted in the model.
